How Relaxed-Fit Outerwear Is Supposed to Fit

15 August 2026

Relaxed fit is the most inconsistently applied label in clothing. One brand's relaxed is another's regular. The letters on the tag mean nothing across brands, which is why the only reliable method is measurements.

What relaxed fit actually describes

Relaxed is not simply scaled-up. It is a different set of proportions:

  • Wider through the chest and body, so the garment hangs away from you rather than following your shape.
  • A dropped or extended shoulder, so the seam sits past your actual shoulder point.
  • A shorter body relative to the width, which is what stops it reading as oversized rather than relaxed.
  • Roomier sleeves, cut for layering underneath.

That shorter-and-wider ratio is the whole silhouette. Sizing up in a regular-fit jacket gets you width but also length, which is why it ends up looking like a borrowed jacket instead.

The only reliable way to buy

Ignore the letter. Measure a jacket you already own and like, and compare it to the size chart.

Chest is the measurement that matters most. Lay the jacket flat, fastened, and measure straight across from armpit seam to armpit seam. Double it. That figure is what brands publish as chest — most charts, including ours, quote the full circumference, so a 46" chest is a 23" flat measurement.

Length is measured from the highest point of the shoulder straight down to the hem. This one decides whether a jacket sits at your waistband or below it, and it is the measurement people most often ignore and most often regret.

Sleeve is usually from the shoulder seam to the cuff — but on a dropped shoulder that seam already sits lower on your arm, so a relaxed jacket's sleeve number is not comparable to a tailored one's.

Reading a size chart properly

To take our own as an example: a small in the cotton-bodied jackets is 46" chest and 25" length, stepping to 48"/26" for medium and 50"/27" for large. The sherpa runs wider at the same nominal size — 49" chest at small — because the pile itself takes up room.

Two useful things fall out of that. First, a 2" chest step between sizes is small; if you are between sizes, the decision is about how you want it to sit, not about fitting. Second, the same letter differs across fabrics within one brand, let alone between brands.

Sizing up or down

Take your true size if you want the silhouette as designed. Size down for a cleaner, closer line — this works for structured cotton and corduroy, where the fabric holds shape on its own. Avoid sizing down in high-loft sherpa, where the pile needs the room and a snug fit looks compressed.

Size up only if you plan to layer heavily underneath. Relaxed fit already includes layering room, so sizing up on top of that usually reads as oversized.

The check before you buy

Take the jacket you reach for most, measure it flat, and write down chest, length and sleeve. Keep it in your phone. It turns every size chart from guesswork into arithmetic — and it is the single most useful thing you can do when buying clothing you cannot try on.
