How to Wash Embroidered and Jacquard Knitwear Without Wrecking It

15 August 2026

A knit sweater with embroidery or jacquard work has two materials behaving differently in the same wash: a stretchy knitted ground and a stable, denser decoration stitched through it. Most damage comes from treating it like a t-shirt.

The three ways it goes wrong

Stretching. Wet knitwear is heavy, and knit structure has no memory when loaded. Hang a soaked sweater and gravity pulls the shoulders and body permanently longer. This is the most common and least reversible failure.

Puckering. Embroidery thread and knit yarn shrink at different rates. Heat exaggerates the difference, so the ground gathers around the stitching and the motif distorts.

Colour bleed. Multi-colour jacquard puts saturated yarns directly against pale ones. Warm water lifts dye and the pale yarn takes it up. Reds and deep blues are the usual culprits.

The method

  1. Turn it inside out. Abrasion happens on whatever faces outward. Inside out puts the wear on the plain reverse rather than on the embroidery.
  2. Cold water only. Cold prevents both shrinkage mismatch and dye migration. There is no washing benefit to warm water on knitwear.
  3. Hand wash, or machine on delicate. If machine washing, use a mesh bag. The agitator is what catches and pulls stitching.
  4. Mild detergent, no bleach, no fabric softener. Softener coats fibres and dulls jacquard colour over time. Bleach destroys wool.
  5. Do not wring. Press the water out between towels. Twisting is what breaks knit structure.
  6. Dry flat. This is the important one. Lay it on a towel and reshape it to its measurements while damp. Never hang, never tumble dry.

Ironing

Short answer: do not iron directly over embroidery, jacquard or print. Heat and pressure flatten stitching permanently and can melt polyester embroidery thread.

If you must, turn the garment inside out, use a pressing cloth, and keep the iron off the decorated area entirely. Steaming from a distance is safer than contact.

Storage

Fold knitwear. Do not hang it. A hanger concentrates the whole garment's weight on two points and the shoulders deform over months. Fold it, and store somewhere dry — damp is what attracts moths to wool blends, not the wool itself.

How often to wash

Less than you think. Knitwear is usually worn over a base layer, so it is not in direct contact with skin for most of its wear. Airing it overnight resolves most of what you would wash it for. Every few wears is plenty; every wear shortens its life for no benefit.
